# Keep Graphify as locator, not answerer

At the pinned 0.9.95, Graphify can find which notes matter. It cannot hand an answering model any fact text, and it cannot propose typed, justified links. The second limit is fixed in its source, not in its configuration. Every released read surface (`query`, `explain`, `path`, the MCP tools, wiki and Obsidian exports) prints labels, relations, confidences and source paths, and never a note's body, frontmatter or rationale ([PKG serve.py, cli.py, wiki.py, export.py](file:///C:/Users/shman/AppData/Roaming/uv/tools/graphifyy/Lib/site-packages/graphify/serve.py)). The headless extraction prompt is a module constant. It limits edges to seven generic verbs and asks for rationale only when a note states an explicit "why" ([PKG llm.py L502-557](file:///C:/Users/shman/AppData/Roaming/uv/tools/graphifyy/Lib/site-packages/graphify/llm.py)). That fully explains our 7/35 label-only score, the 54 of 57 generic edges and the 0 of 43 rationales. No open upstream PR changes the verb vocabulary. The PRs that would help retrieval (rationale rendering, frontmatter seeding, structural markdown routing) are all still unmerged into `v8`, the branch releases are cut from. The wider evidence agrees with what we measured. On entity-anchored, 1–2-hop factual questions, plain retrieval matches or beats LLM-built graphs. Graph wins show up only on multi-hop, temporal, comparison and corpus-wide "themes" questions over large, unlinked corpora. Our 42-note brain is about 10k tokens, roughly 1/20th of the size at which Anthropic says to drop retrieval and put the whole knowledge base in a cached prompt. The efficient, accurate path is to keep our own pipeline for answers, keep Graphify (if at all) as an unmodified note locator, and move link proposal to our own deterministic scan plus a schema-constrained verifier. Which role Graphify keeps is Manoj's call under D32.

## Graphify 0.9.95 prints skeletons, by design

Every way of reading the graph leads to the same renderer. `graphify query` and MCP `query_graph` emit `NODE <label> [src= loc= community=]` lines, then `EDGE` lines. An `attrs={…}` field appears only when a node has an `attributes` dict, and in the installed code only the Terraform extractor writes one ([PKG serve.py L1177-1246](file:///C:/Users/shman/AppData/Roaming/uv/tools/graphifyy/Lib/site-packages/graphify/serve.py)). Rationale is read, but only to score seeds, through a 0.75 bonus tier. It is never printed ([PKG serve.py L338-346, L645, L708-711](file:///C:/Users/shman/AppData/Roaming/uv/tools/graphifyy/Lib/site-packages/graphify/serve.py)). `explain`, `get_node`, `get_neighbors` and `path` add degree, file type and community, but no body text ([PKG cli.py L2092-2190](file:///C:/Users/shman/AppData/Roaming/uv/tools/graphifyy/Lib/site-packages/graphify/cli.py)). The wiki and Obsidian exporters are built purely from the graph, so their notes are "bare stubs" until users wire the sources back in by hand ([#1968](https://github.com/Graphify-Labs/graphify/issues/1968); [chaseai.io](https://www.chaseai.io/blog/graphify-obsidian-claude-code-second-brain)). **Our 7/35 is not a tuning failure. It is the designed output of a navigation tool.** The project's own query skill tells the agent to cite `source_location` and go back to the sources ([PKG query.md L75-76](file:///C:/Users/shman/AppData/Roaming/uv/tools/graphifyy/Lib/site-packages/graphify/skills/claude/references/query.md)).

Two hidden traps distort any natural-language use of `query` on our notes. First, `_infer_context_filters` scans the question for words such as "returned", "module", "called" or "property". When one appears, it keeps only edges whose `context` matches. LLM and markdown edges never carry `context`, so the traversal silently collapses to the seed nodes. The only signal is a `Context: … (heuristic)` header ([PKG serve.py L906-1008](file:///C:/Users/shman/AppData/Roaming/uv/tools/graphifyy/Lib/site-packages/graphify/serve.py)). The bug is still open ([#2529](https://github.com/Graphify-Labs/graphify/issues/2529)), and Apollo questions about who "returned to Earth" or the "command module" trigger it. Second, the budget is about 3 chars per token, and seed nodes render first. On a 43-node graph, a depth-2 BFS reaches most of the graph, so `--budget 2000` fills with label lines, and a bigger budget adds only more labels ([PKG serve.py L1131-1308](file:///C:/Users/shman/AppData/Roaming/uv/tools/graphifyy/Lib/site-packages/graphify/serve.py)). Matching is case-folded substring plus IDF, with "no stemming, no synonyms" ([PKG query.md L25, L48](file:///C:/Users/shman/AppData/Roaming/uv/tools/graphifyy/Lib/site-packages/graphify/skills/claude/references/query.md)). Some of the 10 points between our 25/35 hybrid and our 35/35 pipeline are therefore plausibly seed misses and filter collapse, not a lack of facts. That has not been measured yet, and it is experiment 3 below.

Link proposal is limited even harder. The headless prompt's edge schema allows only `calls|implements|references|cites|conceptually_related_to|shares_data_with|semantically_similar_to`. It has no edge `context` field and no node `attributes` field. It also tells the model to connect a linked file with `references` or `cites` ([PKG llm.py L521-541](file:///C:/Users/shman/AppData/Roaming/uv/tools/graphifyy/Lib/site-packages/graphify/llm.py)). No flag, environment variable or `.graphifyrc` key injects prompt text. `--mode deep` appends a suffix that says to *avoid* broad similarity edges, and `--token-budget` and `--max-concurrency` only change packing and parallelism ([PKG llm.py L544-557, L2285-2801](file:///C:/Users/shman/AppData/Roaming/uv/tools/graphifyy/Lib/site-packages/graphify/llm.py); [PKG rcfile.py](file:///C:/Users/shman/AppData/Roaming/uv/tools/graphifyy/Lib/site-packages/graphify/rcfile.py)). The maintainer describes the enum as chosen to span code, docs and papers, with `conceptually_related_to` as "soft semantic affinity when no harder edge fits" ([#377](https://github.com/Graphify-Labs/graphify/issues/377)). **A "replaced by" or "commanded" verb cannot come out of `graphify extract` unmodified, at this or any foreseeable version.** Rationale behaves the same way: it fills only when a note states a reason, and our notes are mostly one-line facts, which is why 0 of 43 nodes got one.

Graphify has one released capability that fits us well and that we have not used. `graphify update <path>` runs the deterministic `extract_markdown` parser with no LLM. It turns every `[[wikilink]]`, including those in frontmatter, into an EXTRACTED `references` edge, and parses frontmatter onto the page node ([PKG extractors/markdown.py L369-577](file:///C:/Users/shman/AppData/Roaming/uv/tools/graphifyy/Lib/site-packages/graphify/extractors/markdown.py); [PKG watch.py L1827-1900](file:///C:/Users/shman/AppData/Roaming/uv/tools/graphifyy/Lib/site-packages/graphify/watch.py)). It drops the verb text and the fact lines. It would still give an exact, free, repeatable map of the authored link structure, against the LLM path's 43-vs-72-node swing between two identical runs ([link-recovery README](file:///C:/Users/shman/OneDrive/Desktop/Yggdrass%20OS/Yggdrass-OS/evals/experiments/link-recovery/README.md)). `graphify extract`, by contrast, classes `.md` as documents and sends them all to the model, which is what happened in our runs ([PKG detect.py L49](file:///C:/Users/shman/AppData/Roaming/uv/tools/graphifyy/Lib/site-packages/graphify/detect.py); [#2383](https://github.com/Graphify-Labs/graphify/issues/2383)).

## Evidence says graphs lose on lookups at our scale

The best-controlled studies point one way. In Han et al.'s systematic comparison, plain RAG beat every GraphRAG variant on NQ single-hop (F1 64.78 vs 34.28 for triplets-only and 63.01 for community-local, 8B). Graph methods won only on MultiHop-RAG's temporal (49.06 vs 25.73) and comparison queries. A token-matched RAG baseline then erased most of that gain (71.01 vs 71.17) ([Han et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v2)). The same study found **only about 66% of answer entities present in the LLM-extracted KG**, and recovered much of the loss by attaching source text back to triples (NQ 70B: 28.50 → 53.88 F1) ([Han et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v2)). That is the same lift we got going from 7/35 to 25/35 by sending fact lines. GraphRAG-Bench (ICLR 2026) shows the same split. On level-1 fact retrieval, RAG plus rerank scored 60.92 against MS-GraphRAG's 49.29. On complex reasoning, HippoRAG2 more than doubled RAG's score, and per-query tokens ranged from 879 (vanilla) to 331,375 (MS-GraphRAG global) ([Xiang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690v3)). The large early "wins" were inflated by judges. Swapping answer order moved LightRAG's self-vs-self win rate by more than 30%, and a 25-token length gap swung win rates by more than 50% ([Zeng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06331)).

Graphify's own evidence on non-code corpora is weak. The 71.5x figure is a token ratio on a 126-file codebase, with no accuracy measured ([lucasrosati/claude-code-memory-setup](https://github.com/lucasrosati/claude-code-memory-setup)). BENCHMARKS.md's LOCOMO result (45.3% vs 43.3% hybrid RRF) comes from an unreleased harness that uses BGE-m3 embeddings and no LLM at ingest. It is not CLI `query` ([BENCHMARKS.md](https://github.com/Graphify-Labs/graphify/blob/v8/BENCHMARKS.md); [#1706](https://github.com/Graphify-Labs/graphify/issues/1706)). The closest analogue to our test, run on a vault, found BM25 precision@5 of **0.627 against 0.107 for `explain`** ([#3143](https://github.com/Graphify-Labs/graphify/issues/3143)). On SWE-bench Lite, `query_graph` put the right file first 25% of the time against BM25's 32% ([#4341](https://github.com/Graphify-Labs/graphify/issues/4341)). In a two-agent comparison, `query` used more tool calls and tokens than plain Read/Grep, because node text is "a paraphrase, not a citable quote" ([#3308](https://github.com/Graphify-Labs/graphify/issues/3308)). No published accuracy evaluation of Graphify `query` on markdown notes exists.

Human links are the stronger backbone. Retrievers that walk Wikipedia's human-authored hyperlinks beat the prior best on HotpotQA by more than 14 points ([Asai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.10470)). On a 566-file Obsidian vault, a regex wikilink pass produced 10.4 edges per file in under 2 s, against 7.3 from the LLM ([#295](https://github.com/Graphify-Labs/graphify/issues/295)). Separately, an LLM-only pipeline over 1,256 help articles left 56% of nodes isolated ([#951](https://github.com/Graphify-Labs/graphify/issues/951)). Scale settles it. Our brain is about 41k characters across 43 files, roughly 10k tokens at 4 chars/token. That is far under the 200k-token line below which Anthropic recommends putting the whole knowledge base in a cached prompt, with caching cutting cost by up to 90% ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)). At about 240 tokens per note, that line sits near **800 notes** (an estimate from this corpus, not a measured threshold). The 35/35 pipeline already does what the literature recommends: authored links for hops, source text attached, one bounded expansion. Iterative graph walking pays only on hard multi-hop sets (MuSiQue +9 to +16 SubEM), and papers do not report its cost per correct answer ([GraphSearch](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22009)).

One honest caveat cuts the other way. Our 35-question eval is entity-anchored and mostly 1–2 hops, the question type where graphs are predicted to add nothing. With 35 questions, 35/35 and 34/35 are within noise (95% CI about ±6 points). Questions that share a source note need clustered standard errors, which Anthropic found run more than 3x naive ones ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/statistical-approach-to-model-evals)). The gap of 10 between 25 and 35 is real. A "graphs don't help" conclusion still holds only for the questions we have tested.

## The best link proposers are cheap and schema-bound

On link proposal, our own data already shows a cheaper option. The free roster scan (Arm A) recovered **11 of 20 deleted pairs in both runs, with zero noise, at $0 and about 1 s**. Every proposal came with the fact line that justified it. Graphify recovered 9 and 7 pairs at about $0.30–0.35 and 182–265 s, with no reasons. Adding a cross-batch model pass (Arm C) on top of Graphify reached 12 of 20 at about $0.44 ([link-recovery README](file:///C:/Users/shman/OneDrive/Desktop/Yggdrass%20OS/Yggdrass-OS/evals/experiments/link-recovery/README.md)). A's weakness is precision: 6 of its 34 new pairs were spurious name matches ("Cape Kennedy" to JFK, the `apollo-11s` possessive), each visible in its own reason line. A gap between the two benchmarks also deserves a check. Graphify's 45 of 81 pairs recovered with 0 wrong ([link-scores.json](file:///C:/Users/shman/OneDrive/Desktop/Yggdrass%20OS/Yggdrass-OS/evals/experiments/graphify-vs-baseline/link-scores.json)) were judged on pairs, not verbs. 48 of those 57 edges were `conceptually_related_to`, so none of them is usable as a typed Yggdras link without a second step that assigns the verb.

Among the alternatives, no tool proposes typed, justified links into a review queue out of the box. Three supply the missing pieces. LlamaIndex's `SchemaLLMPathExtractor(strict=True)` rejects any triple outside a declared relation list, which is a hard vocabulary constraint ([LlamaIndex guide](https://developers.llamaindex.ai/python/framework/module_guides/indexing/lpg_index_guide/)). Graphiti stores a natural-language `fact` and the source episodes on every edge, which is a ready-made justification. Its custom edge types are soft, however, and its PostHog telemetry is on by default ([Graphiti edges.py](https://github.com/getzep/graphiti/blob/main/graphiti_core/edges.py); [Graphiti docs](https://help.getzep.com/graphiti/core-concepts/custom-entity-and-edge-types); [telemetry.py](https://github.com/getzep/graphiti/blob/main/graphiti_core/telemetry/telemetry.py)). Basic Memory's note format (frontmatter, `- [category] fact` observations, `- verb [[Target]]` relations) is almost exactly ours. It indexes deterministically with opt-in telemetry, but under AGPL-3.0, and it proposes nothing ([NOTE-FORMAT.md](https://github.com/basicmachines-co/basic-memory/blob/main/docs/NOTE-FORMAT.md); [telemetry.py](https://github.com/basicmachines-co/basic-memory/blob/main/src/basic_memory/telemetry.py)). The GraphRAG family (MS GraphRAG, LightRAG, HippoRAG 2, Cognee) ignores wikilinks and frontmatter and rebuilds an entity graph from chunks. It answers a problem we do not have. Mem0 OSS dropped graph memory in v3 ([Mem0 migration](https://github.com/mem0ai/mem0/blob/main/docs/migration/oss-v2-to-v3.mdx)). The research literature supports the same shape: open extraction followed by canonicalization against a schema (EDC), entity clustering (KGGen), and checking that every extracted entity appears in the source text (RAKG) ([survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.20345); [KGGen](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09956v2); [RAKG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09823)). For us that pipeline is small: run the roster scan for candidates, then one cheap model call per batch that either picks a verb from the Yggdras vocabulary, quoting the fact line, or rejects the pair. No new framework is needed, and Graphify is not involved.

Experiment 1 comes first because it attacks the one real gap at almost no cost, and it either confirms or kills the idea that Graphify is needed for link proposal. Experiment 2 comes second because 35/35 on easy questions leaves no headroom to measure anything else. Without harder questions, every retrieval experiment is ceiling-bound. Experiments 3 and 4 decide which Graphify roles are worth keeping. Experiments 5 and 6 are cheap insurance with low expected gain.

## Conclusion

The question was never really whether Graphify could be tuned into a better answer engine. Its source shows it was built to point, not to quote, and its extraction prompt cannot speak our verbs. Every point we gained (7 → 25 → 35) came from putting our own fact lines and our own authored links in front of the model. That is the same move the literature credits with rescuing LLM-built graphs. For a hand-linked brain of this size, the authored wikilinks are the knowledge graph. Graphify's best remaining contribution is the cheap, deterministic structural map that `graphify update` already provides, and possibly seeding, if experiment 3 shows that the selector gap was the filter bug rather than Graphify's ranking.

The work with the most leverage is link curation, not retrieval. A free scan already finds more deleted links than Graphify, with a reason attached to each one. A single schema-bound verifier call could turn those candidates into typed proposals that Manoj can review. If that holds, D32 does not need amending. Graphify simply stops being on the link-proposal path, and its pin becomes a question about the map rather than about intelligence. The open unknown is whether harder questions exist where any graph earns its keep. Until a held-out set says so, more graph machinery has no measured payoff.
