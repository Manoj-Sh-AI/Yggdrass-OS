---
name: plan-week
description: Plan the coming week around fixed goals, adapting execution (push, split, pause, stack routines) while protecting essentials.
domains: [tasks, calendar]
---

Goal: a week plan Manoj can actually execute. Goals are fixed; how and when they get done adapts.

1. Read before planning. Read this week's calendar (fixed events, travel, deadlines) and the open
   tasks with their goals, due dates and estimates. Never invent a date, duration or estimate:
   if one is missing, say so and plan around the gap.
2. Protect essentials first. Sleep, meals, exercise, the job's fixed hours, German lessons and any
   event marked fixed are placed before anything else and are never moved to make room.
3. Place goal work into the free blocks that remain, hardest work in the earliest high-focus
   block of the day. One main goal block per day; the rest is small tasks.
4. Adapt execution, never the goal:
   - push: a task that does not fit moves to the next free block, and its due date stays visible;
   - split: work over 2 hours becomes parts of at most 90 minutes, each with a concrete next step;
   - pause: a task blocked by someone else waits, with the person and the date to check again;
   - stack routines: short recurring routines (review, inbox, admin) are grouped back to back
     into one block, so they do not scatter across the day.
5. Leave slack. Keep at least one free hour per day unplanned; a full week breaks on day one.
6. Propose, never save. Every calendar or task change is a proposal Manoj reviews. Say in one
   line what moved and why ("German moved to Thursday: Tuesday has the deadline").

Done when: every essential is placed, every goal has a block or a stated reason it does not,
nothing was moved without a reason, and all changes are proposals.
